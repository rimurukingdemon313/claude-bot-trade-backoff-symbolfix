# Full-system audit, September 2026

A file-by-file pass over `bot/` (19,471 lines) looking for anything that
loses trades, fabricates a number, or claims more than the evidence
supports. What follows is what was found, what was changed, and what was
deliberately left alone.

Nothing in this document is a claim about future profitability (rule 13).

---

## 1. The finding that mattered: the stop did not know about the spread

`bot/smc/engine.py` built its stop as:

```python
buffer = atr_value * 0.2
```

A pure volatility buffer, with no spread term anywhere in it. The same
omission existed independently in `bot/strategy/reversion.py`.

This is not a stylistic gap. A broker-side stop is **not evaluated
against the candle series it was read off**:

| Position | Closed by | Triggers on |
|---|---|---|
| Long | selling | the **bid** |
| Short | buying | the **ask** |

The chart is one of bid, mid or ask. So on at least one side of every
trade, the stop fires while the visible price is still a full spread
short of the invalidation level — the trade is closed for being wrong
before the market has said it is wrong.

### The trade that proves it

A short was closed at `0.82131`. Nothing in the market traded within
**6.1 pips** of that price. Measured on the bid the position was
**+5.8 pips**; it was booked at **−7.3**. The entire 13.1-pip swing was
the spread, and the structural buffer of 0.2 ATR was a fraction of the
spread at that moment.

That is also the answer to "the stop was hit but the candle never moved."
The candle didn't. The ask did.

### The fix

`RiskConfig.stop_spread_multiple` (default `1.0`, env
`RISK_STOP_SPREAD_MULTIPLE`). The live spread is read once per symbol per
scan and threaded into level construction, where it pads the structural
buffer.

Three properties worth stating plainly:

- **Risk in money does not change.** The risk engine sizes from the stop
  distance, so a wider stop buys fewer lots for the same dollars. This is
  asserted by `test_widening_the_stop_does_not_widen_the_money_at_risk`.
- **R:R gets honestly worse**, and some setups now fail the reward floor.
  That is the mechanism working: a setup that cannot pay for its own stop
  once the spread is counted was never the setup it appeared to be.
- **Both directions are padded**, not just shorts. We have not measured
  whether this broker quotes history on the bid, the mid or the ask, and
  padding both is the choice that does not depend on knowing. The new
  `spread_samples` table stores bid and ask beside each reading, which is
  what will eventually answer it — at which point the padding can become
  one-sided and slightly cheaper.

`None` means the quote could not be read. It builds the old, unpadded
stop and says so; it never substitutes a zero (rule 6). A negative
spread from a crossed book is ignored rather than allowed to make a stop
*tighter*.

## 2. The boundary bug the fix exposed

Widening the stop pushed the primary test fixture onto the reward floor
exactly, and it was refused. Investigating that turned up a real defect
rather than a fixture problem.

The target is selected at `min_distance = minimum_reward + offset` and
then has that same `offset` subtracted. A setup landing exactly on the
floor therefore arrives as `1.2` minus one float ulp.

Three of the system's R comparisons already tolerated this:

| Location | Had `- R_EPSILON` |
|---|---|
| `risk/engine.py:373` | yes |
| `strategy/reversion.py:203` | yes |
| `execution/manager.py` (×3) | yes |
| `smc/engine.py` R:R gate | **no** |
| `scoring/scorer.py` R:R component | **no** |
| `risk/reward.py` | **no** |

The scorer's omission was the worse of the three: `risk_reward` is a
CRITICAL component, so a zero there is not a low score, it is an
unconditional veto. A setup on the floor was thrown out as though it had
**no target at all**.

Harmless while stops were narrow enough that nothing landed on the
boundary. The spread term put a setup there and it failed immediately.
All three now use the constant that exists for exactly this.

## 3. We were blind to the only thing we had proven kills us

```
grep "spread" bot/storage/db.py  →  nothing
```

The spread is the one mechanism demonstrated to destroy our trades, and
the system had never written a single one down. Every diagnosis of it has
been one moment's screenshot argued against another moment's measurement
— including one I got wrong in exactly that way.

New `spread_samples` table (schema v7) and `SpreadRepository`. One row per
symbol per scan: bid, ask, spread, session, hour.

It answers, with arithmetic instead of argument:

- **which symbols are chronically expensive.** A symbol whose median
  spread is a large fraction of a typical stop is not a symbol with
  occasional bad luck. No amount of setup quality fixes it — the only
  fix is to stop trading it.
- **which hours are.** The rollover blackout is currently 45/30 minutes
  around 21:00 UTC, set from *one* live observation. This makes that an
  argument from data.
- **which price the candles are quoted on**, which decides how much
  padding a stop actually needs.

Two deliberate design points:

- Sampling happens **before** the news, data and session gates, not after.
  A spread recorded only on scans that reached the strategy would be a
  sample of the calm hours — stale data, news blackouts and thin sessions
  all correlate with the wide spreads this table exists to catch. Record
  everything; filter in the query.
- A summary over fewer than 30 samples is labelled `insufficient` rather
  than quoted as fact (rule 6 applies to statistics).

Nothing here gates a trade. It is instrumentation.

## 4. `rMultiple: 0.0` meant "unknown"

`PositionManager.track` computed:

```python
current_r = r_multiple(...) if stop and price else 0.0
```

Two functions below it, `_current_price` goes to real trouble to return
`None` with an explanation rather than invent a price — and then the R
derived from that missing price was invented anyway.

`0.0` does not read as "unknown". It reads as **exactly break-even**,
which is the calmest number that field can display, shown precisely when
nothing can be measured. Now `None`, which the dashboard already renders
as `—`.

The same function assumed `entry_price` is always usable.
`bot/broker/tradelocker.py:947` parses it as
`float(_num(...) or 0.0)`, so a position the broker reported without an
`avgPrice` would arrive with an entry of zero. Every number in the row is
measured *from* the entry, so that does not produce a slightly wrong R —
it produces an R in the thousands and an excursion equal to the whole
price of the instrument. Such a position is now reported as unmeasurable.

**Not fixed, recorded here:** `BrokerPosition.entry_price` is still typed
`float` and still defaults to `0.0` at the parser. The honest type is
`float | None`. That change touches eight call sites in the live
management path and was judged too risky to make in the same pass as the
stop change — one meaningful thing at a time (rule 9). The consumer now
fails closed, so the fabricated zero can no longer produce a fabricated
measurement.

## 5. "A grades only", named rather than remembered

`SCORING_MIN_TRADEABLE=68` already worked. It is also a number an
operator has to know, and has to remember to change again if the band
ever moves.

New `SCORING_MIN_TIER`, accepting `A+`, `A` or `B`. Where both are set,
the stricter wins — neither can be used to talk the other down. An
unrecognised value is **refused at startup**, because silently defaulting
to B would mean an operator who typed `A1` ran a fortnight believing they
had filtered by grade. That is the same failure the previously-dead knob
produced, and it should not be reintroduced in a friendlier spelling.

### What the evidence actually says about A-only

From the one generated dataset we have, 180 trades:

| Grade | n | win rate | avg R |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 54 | 46.3% | **+0.014** |
| B | 119 | 28.6% | **−0.152** |

The entire loss lived in the B cohort. That is the strongest argument
available for `SCORING_MIN_TIER=A`.

**And the argument against it, which is real:** the correlation between
the *continuous* score and the outcome was **+0.037** — near enough to
nothing. One threshold doing real work while the arithmetic behind it
ranks almost at random is a strange pair of facts. It is one dataset, and
a generated one.

Setting it cuts roughly **70% of trades**. Expect long stretches of
nothing. That is the intended behaviour, not a fault (rule 8).

## 6. What a serious systematic desk does that we now do too

Taken honestly rather than as a list of things to copy:

| Practice | Status |
|---|---|
| Cost modelled at the decision, not just the fill | **now done** (§1) — this was the gap |
| Every execution-cost observation stored | **now done** (§3) |
| Unknown reported as unknown, never as zero | rule 6, enforced; two more holes closed (§4) |
| Pre-registered selection rules, validated out-of-sample | already done — `docs/EXPERIMENT_*.md`, including one that **failed** validation and is recorded as failed |
| Single sizing authority | already done — rule 2, `risk/engine.py` |
| No write retries without an idempotency key | already done — rule 3 |
| Strictly causal backtest | already done — rule 4, `confirmed_index` |
| Strategy version stamped on every trade | already done — rule 9 |
| Small samples labelled inconclusive | already done — rule 13 |

The gap was never sophistication. It was that the most expensive thing in
retail FX — the spread — was modelled in the fill and absent from the
decision.

### What was deliberately NOT added

Rule 12 says an indicator earns its place only by adding independent
information *and* improving out-of-sample robustness. Nothing was added
on the grounds that other systems have it. Specifically rejected this
pass: additional confirmation indicators, a volatility-regime filter
beyond the existing one, and any parameter whose only justification would
be a backtest it was fitted to.

## 7. What this audit cannot tell you

The backtester requires broker credentials by design — it refuses to run
on substitute data, because backtesting prices you will not trade is a
simulation of a different market. So **the changes in §1 and §2 have not
been measured against price history in this pass.** They are sound by
construction and covered by 17 new behavioural tests; that is a different
and weaker claim than "measured".

The honest standard remains the one in `docs/WHEN_IS_IT_DONE.md`: change
nothing, let ~20 trades close, then read the record. The spread table
means that reading will, for the first time, include the variable that
has done the damage.
